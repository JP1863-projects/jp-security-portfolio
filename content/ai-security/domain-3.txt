@3.1|Lineage and accountability

What governs the decision?|Verify underlying rights and restrictions before use|Rely solely on the internal approval|Treat encryption as permission|Treat aggregation as unrestricted use|Internal approval cannot create rights absent from the underlying data or override applicable restrictions.

A data steward and business owner disagree about allowable use. What is the appropriate next step?|Use documented authority and escalation to resolve the restriction|Let the pipeline proceed by default|Treat the steward as the model developer|Use the newest dataset automatically|Defined roles and escalation should resolve purpose and accountability before disputed processing continues.

What should be documented?|Acquisition details, hashes, custody transfers, and storage controls|The snapshot's display name|Its average record length|The model accuracy score|Evidence handling requires traceability and integrity across acquisition, transfers, and analysis.
A source data restriction changes after training. A catalog labels an entire mixed dataset public despite restricted records. A data owner approves a purpose but no pipeline control enforces it. A team adds differential privacy noise but ignores repeated releases. A developer masks names but keeps free-text notes unchanged. A privacy team needs aggregate statistics without revealing a particular individual's contribution. A user withdraws permission for an optional processing purpose. A model memorizes rare sensitive records despite masked names. A health assistant only needs age range and symptom category. A team wants to test whether synthetic data leaks training examples. A privacy notice covers analytics but the organization adds model training. What is the first review question?|Whether the new purpose has an appropriate legal basis and notice|Whether training uses a GPU|Whether the notice has a search box|Whether the dataset has more rows|Purpose changes require factual privacy analysis; technical method alone does not establish permission.

A training bucket allows every developer to overwrite approved data. What should improve?|Restricted write access and controlled version promotion|Read-only access for the training service only|A longer bucket description|More frequent unreviewed overwrites|Controlled writes and versioned approvals reduce unauthorized modifications; restricting readers alone leaves tampering possible.
A team receives a signed dataset from a vendor. Which review remains necessary?|Validate content suitability and behavior-related risks|Skip all data-quality checks|Assume no harmful records exist|Treat the signature as permission for every use|A trusted signature supports origin and integrity; it does not establish that content is safe or appropriate.
A new dataset dramatically changes a model's behavior on one customer segment. A poisoning incident is suspected in the latest training data. A team trains on user feedback without vetting it. What risk deserves attention?|Adversarial feedback influencing learning behavior|Guaranteed elimination of bias|Automatic verification of labels|Elimination of distribution shift|Feedback is an input channel that can be manipulated; establish quality, trust, and abuse controls.
A pipeline's quality gate checks only total row count. What should it also assess?|Schema, distributions, labels, provenance, and suspicious changes|Whether filenames are short|The dataset's download popularity|The team's preferred model|Counts cannot reveal many integrity or quality failures; validation should reflect credible manipulation paths.
A contributor can alter both a dataset and its reference hash. What does a matching hash prove?|Little about integrity without an independently trusted reference|That tampering was impossible|That the contributor had business approval|That the data is unbiased|An attacker controlling content and reference can make them agree; protect the trusted comparison separately.
A team mixes unvetted generated examples into a validated corpus. What should occur before promotion?|Assess provenance, quality, contamination, and resulting behavior|Assume generated examples are clean|Deduplicate filenames|Increase training duration|Generated content can introduce errors or compromised patterns; validate the change as learning material.
@3.4|Cryptography and protection
An application stores its data-encryption key beside the encrypted training set with identical access. What is the weakness?|A single access compromise may expose both key and data|Encryption cannot work on datasets|The key is necessarily mathematically weak|The dataset becomes anonymous|Separation and controlled key access reduce the chance that one storage compromise defeats encryption.
A service rotates an exposed API key but leaves old copies valid. What should happen?|Revoke compromised credentials and validate the replacement workflow|Keep both keys indefinitely for convenience|Rename the new credential|Clear browser history|Rotation is incomplete if exposed credentials still authorize access; revoke them and confirm dependent services work safely.
A team uses TLS for inference traffic but logs raw requests publicly. What remains exposed?|Sensitive content through the logging path|Packet headers|Encrypted payload length|Nothing because TLS was enabled|Transport protection applies to transit; plaintext logs create a separate disclosure path.
A model endpoint should reject bulk requests containing secrets. Which control is relevant?|Content-aware input controls combined with access and usage policy|Faster tokenization|A larger response buffer|A more permissive endpoint|Inspection can help detect sensitive inputs but should complement authorization, minimization, and clearly defined use.
A team proposes cryptographic erasure of encrypted archives. What must be verified?|All relevant key copies and recovery paths are appropriately destroyed|The primary filename is deleted|The latest backup is renamed|The archive index is hidden|Erasure depends on removing effective decryption capability, including retained keys and recoverable copies.
A provider offers customer-managed keys. What must the customer still assess?|Which data states and services the key actually protects|Assume the provider can never see plaintext|Assume deletion obligations disappear|Assume access reviews are unnecessary|Customer key control can improve governance but its protection scope and runtime access must be understood.
An AI platform decrypts data during computation. A data access catalog grants access by business purpose. What should enforcement verify?|The request's identity, approved purpose, and applicable constraints|The user's claimed purpose in free text|The model's answer confidence|The dataset's popularity|Purpose-based access requires verifiable attributes and policy enforcement, not untrusted assertions.
A backup key is restored after a supposedly completed crypto-erasure.
