@2.1|Governance design
An organization has many AI pilots but no central record. What should governance establish first?|An inventory with owners, purposes, data, and deployment status|A single shared API token|An unapproved model leaderboard|A ban on documenting experiments|An inventory makes use cases and accountability visible; technical standardization alone does not reveal exposure.
A board approves an AI policy. What makes it operational?|Assigned responsibilities, procedures, enforcement, and measures|A policy logo|A model temperature setting|A vendor testimonial|Policy needs implementation and evidence; approval alone does not demonstrate effective governance.
A business unit exempts its AI pilot from review because it is experimental. What matters most?|The pilot's actual data, actions, and potential harm|Whether the word pilot appears|Whether the UI is temporary|Whether the model is small|Experimental status does not remove risk; evaluate actual exposure and consequences.
Two departments disagree about who can approve a high-risk assistant. What should resolve this?|A documented decision-rights and escalation structure|The fastest deployment team|The model's preference|A random tie-breaker|Clear authority and escalation prevent competing assumptions about accountability and risk acceptance.
A company copies its server patch policy as its entire AI policy. What is missing?|Controls for data, behavior, human impact, and model changes|A longer document title|Endpoint operating-system names|Rack diagrams|AI governance includes behavioral and data-related risks beyond traditional software patching.
A policy says all AI use must be explainable. What should implementation clarify?|Audience, purpose, required explanation, and evidence|That every model must expose source code publicly|That confident prose is sufficient|That explanations replace accuracy testing|Explainability requirements depend on decisions and users; plausible language is not evidence of faithful reasoning.
A department buys a chatbot outside procurement. What is the best governance response?|Discover its use, assess exposure, and route it through approval|Ignore it until renewal|Approve it because a card was accepted|Delete all procurement records|Shadow AI needs visibility and proportionate review rather than assumed safety or missing records.
Which governance measure best indicates control implementation?|Sampled use cases meeting documented review requirements|Number of policy pages|Number of AI marketing posts|Average prompt length|Evidence of actual reviews measures implementation more directly than document volume or publicity.
@2.2|Ethics and fairness
A hiring tool has equal overall accuracy but rejects qualified applicants disproportionately in one group. What is needed?|Group-specific error analysis and impact review|Another overall accuracy score|Removal of all appeal channels|Automatic acceptance of vendor fairness claims|Aggregate accuracy can hide unequal error patterns; evaluate the relevant groups and decision consequences.
A fairness fix improves one metric but worsens another. What should the team do?|Document tradeoffs and evaluate them against the use case|Claim all fairness goals are satisfied|Select whichever metric looks best silently|Stop measuring affected groups|Fairness metrics can conflict; accountable decisions require explicit context, tradeoffs, and validation.
An appeal process exists, but affected people cannot discover it. What should improve?|Accessible notice and a usable challenge process|Model size|Inference throughput|Training epochs|A right to challenge is ineffective without practical visibility and access to meaningful review.
An AI system explains a denial using a fluent rationale unsupported by the actual decision process. What is the issue?|The explanation may be misleading rather than faithful|Fluency proves transparency|The model has demonstrated causality|The output is automatically auditable|Generated rationales can sound plausible without reflecting the factors that produced the decision.
A support assistant works poorly for users with speech impairments. What should be evaluated?|Accessibility and performance for affected users|Average response length|System uptime|The most common accent|Inclusive design evaluates who can use the system effectively, not just average performance.
A team removes a protected attribute and declares the model unbiased. What is overlooked?|Other features may act as proxies|All bias requires explicit sensitive fields|Training data becomes random automatically|Feature deletion guarantees equal outcomes|Correlated features can preserve discriminatory patterns; test outcomes and mechanisms after feature changes.
A company deploys a synthetic spokesperson without disclosure. Which governance concern arises?|Potentially misleading presentation of AI-generated identity|Guaranteed model extraction|Unavoidable data poisoning|Automatic encryption failure|Disclosure and audience context matter when synthetic content could mislead people about identity or authenticity.
An AI workload consumes far more compute than expected for a minor benefit. What should governance review?|Proportionality, resource use, and alternatives|The screen resolution|The model nickname|Staff enthusiasm|Responsible adoption considers operational and environmental costs alongside demonstrated benefit and risk.
A model performs well on one language but serves a multilingual population. What is the strongest next step?|Evaluate safety and quality across supported languages|Translate the disclaimer only|Assume English results generalize|Disable user feedback|Different languages can change performance and failure modes; evaluate the actual served population.
A product team wants users to believe an AI agent is a human employee. What should security leadership request?|Transparent identity disclosure and impact assessment|A more convincing fake biography|Removal of audit records|Unrestricted agent authority|Deceptive presentation can undermine informed use and accountability; disclosure must match the interaction context.
@2.3|Risk decisions
Two assistants use the same model, but only one can execute financial changes. Which deserves greater action-risk scrutiny?|The assistant with financial authority|Both solely according to parameter count|The assistant with a shorter name|Neither if the model is commercially sold|Risk depends on authority and consequences, not just the model shared by both systems.
A risk register lists threats without owners or treatments. What should be added?|Accountable owners, actions, due dates, and residual risk|A larger severity font|Vendor model names|Generic AI slogans|Risk tracking should drive accountable treatment and review rather than remain a passive threat list.
An AI team reports a precise loss estimate based on unsupported assumptions. What should reviewers request?|Assumptions, uncertainty ranges, and supporting evidence|More decimal places|A stronger color scale|An assurance that AI calculated it|Quantification should expose uncertainty; numerical precision does not establish evidentiary quality.
A low-risk summarizer will start processing health information. What is the appropriate response?|Reassess data sensitivity and controls before the change|Keep its original classification automatically|Change the product name|Wait until an incident occurs|Changed data sensitivity can alter obligations and impact even when the application function remains similar.
A control reduces likelihood but leaves severe possible harm. What must the approver consider?|Residual risk and whether it is acceptable|The control purchase price|Initial risk before treatment|Whether the model apologizes on failure|Controls rarely remove all risk; decisions must address remaining likelihood and consequences.
A team labels a risk transferred after buying insurance. What remains necessary?|Manage controls and obligations that insurance does not remove|Stop incident response planning|Disable access reviews|Assume all liability disappears|Risk transfer mechanisms may cover specified losses but do not eliminate operational responsibility or every obligation.
An AI use case has no defensible benefit and substantial unresolved harm. Which treatment may be appropriate?|Avoid the use case|Deploy to learn from real harm|Accept automatically because it is innovative|Hide the assessment|Avoidance is a valid treatment when benefits do not justify exposure or acceptable controls are unavailable.
A manager signs a risk exception indefinitely. What would strengthen it?|Time limits, compensating controls, and review triggers|Removal of ownership|A more technical acronym|A promise never to revisit it|Exceptions need bounded scope and reassessment so temporary exposure does not become invisible permanent risk.
A model update changes decision behavior without changing application code. What should risk governance do?|Trigger proportionate reassessment and validation|Assume no change occurred|Update the help text|Wait for the annual budget cycle|Behavioral changes can alter exposure without a code diff; review triggers should include model changes.
A risk committee prioritizes purely by model popularity. What is a stronger basis?|Use-case impact, exposure, likelihood, and control effectiveness|Social media mentions|Average answer verbosity|The most attractive demo|Risk prioritization connects credible threats to business consequences and existing safeguards.
@2.4|Privacy and intellectual property
A dataset is publicly downloadable. What must be checked before commercial training use?|License, permitted purposes, privacy, and other applicable rights|Download speed|Whether it has a CSV extension|Whether its name contains open|Public availability is not evidence of unrestricted licensing or lawful handling of all included data.
A team wants to reuse support transcripts for a new training purpose. What should happen first?|Review purpose compatibility, permissions, and applicable privacy requirements|Assume collection authorizes every future use|Remove the transcript title only|Extend retention without review|Authorization for one purpose does not automatically cover a materially different use of personal data.
A provider retains prompts despite the customer's deletion request. What evidence is needed?|Actual retention terms, deletion scope, and obligations|The size of the model|The provider's office address alone|The UI theme|Deletion requirements depend on actual copies, commitments, and applicable obligations; a button is insufficient evidence.
An engineer pastes export-controlled designs into an unapproved AI service. What should the organization do?|Contain the disclosure and involve the relevant security and compliance owners|Assume generated text cannot retain designs|Delete the browser shortcut|Wait for vendor marketing updates|Sensitive disclosure requires prompt assessment of recipients, retention, and applicable controls by responsible specialists.
A contract prohibits provider training on customer prompts. What should a review verify?|Configuration and handling evidence consistent with the contract|The contract file's existence|The model's response style|The provider's market share|Contractual commitments need operational implementation; documentation alone does not establish actual data handling.
A model reproduces recognizable licensed material. What should the release team do?|Review rights, reproduction risks, and required mitigations|Assume generated output is always unrestricted|Change the output font|Disable copyright review|AI generation does not automatically remove intellectual-property constraints; review the actual material and use.
A product requests consent through a vague all-purpose checkbox. What should privacy reviewers examine?|Whether notice and choice meaningfully cover the intended processing|Checkbox color|Whether the box is preselected for convenience|The model vendor|The quality of consent depends on context, specificity, and applicable requirements rather than interface presence alone.
A dataset combines permitted records with records lacking usage rights. What is the strongest response?|Resolve record-level rights and exclude unapproved material|Treat majority permission as sufficient|Rename the combined dataset|Use stronger compression|Mixing data does not erase restrictions; lineage must support decisions about included material.
A privacy officer asks which subprocessors receive prompts. What should the AI owner provide?|A verified downstream data-flow and subprocessor record|The main vendor's name|Model parameter counts|An abstract architecture slogan|Privacy and supplier assessment require visibility into actual recipients and handling, including subcontracted services.
An AI service's retention setting changes after renewal. What should the owner do?|Compare new terms and settings with approved requirements|Assume renewal preserves all controls|Change the invoice reference|Wait until the next breach|Renewals and provider changes can alter approved handling; governance should detect and assess the difference.
@2.5|Supplier assurance
A vendor supplies an audit report covering a different product. What should reviewers do?|Check scope and obtain evidence relevant to the AI service|Accept the brand's report automatically|Ignore the service architecture|Compare report cover dates|Assurance is meaningful only when its scope, period, and controls apply to the service being adopted.
An AI supplier uses an undisclosed model provider. What risk needs attention?|Unassessed downstream dependencies and responsibilities|Guaranteed better accuracy|Automatic license compatibility|Elimination of confidentiality risk|Hidden dependencies can introduce data handling, continuity, and contractual risks outside the original assessment.
A supplier contract lacks incident notification terms. What should procurement address?|Notification duties, timing expectations, and coordination rights|Dashboard branding|Response temperature|Token discounts|Timely coordinated response requires clear supplier commitments; technical alerts alone may not reveal provider incidents.
An organization uses one model provider for every critical workflow. What should continuity planning assess?|Concentration risk and viable fallback options|The supplier's logo|Total prompt length|Developer satisfaction|A shared dependency can create correlated outages or disruptions across otherwise separate business processes.
A vendor promises zero data retention but keeps diagnostic payloads. What should reviewers resolve?|The exact scope and exceptions of the retention claim|How the claim is displayed|Whether zero appears in the brochure|Whether the model is proprietary|A broad claim can hide exceptions; decisions need the actual handling of prompts, outputs, and diagnostics.
A provider changes its terms to permit additional data use. What should the customer do?|Reassess the change against requirements before continued affected use|Assume old approval covers new purposes|Delete the procurement ticket|Increase request volume|Provider changes can invalidate the assumptions supporting the original approval and require a new decision.
An open model has many downloads but no trustworthy origin evidence. What should determine approval?|Provenance verification and independent validation|Download count alone|The most flattering benchmark|Whether the filename says safe|Popularity does not establish artifact integrity, rights, or behavior under the intended conditions.
A supplier's availability SLA excludes the outage type your workflow relies on. What should be done?|Assess the uncovered dependency and negotiate or design mitigation|Treat the headline SLA as full coverage|Ignore the exclusion|Disable failover tests|Assurance depends on actual contractual coverage and technical recovery, not a headline percentage.
A shared-responsibility diagram assigns every control to the provider. What should the customer verify?|Its own integration, access, data, and usage responsibilities|That the vendor has a large support team|The region name|Whether the diagram is colorful|Managed services do not remove customer responsibility for configuration, permissions, and appropriate data use.
A team requests an AI bill of materials. What should it help identify?|Models, data and software dependencies with relevant origins and versions|Employee contact details|Monthly token spend|Generated marketing copy|A useful component inventory supports dependency risk analysis; it is not by itself proof of secure behavior.
@2.6|Oversight and auditing
An AI reviewer approves hundreds of actions without seeing the supporting evidence. What should change?|Provide decision evidence and realistic review capacity|Increase the approval quota|Hide uncertainty to save time|Automatically approve older requests|Oversight is meaningful only when reviewers have evidence, authority, and time to assess proposed actions.
A human can stop an autonomous process only after irreversible harm. What should design review consider?|Earlier intervention points and bounded authority|A louder post-event alarm|A more detailed apology|A faster model|Intervention must occur before unacceptable consequences when practical; delayed visibility is not effective prevention.
An audit finds approvals without model versions. What is the main weakness?|Decisions cannot be tied reliably to deployed behavior|The UI cannot support dark mode|The model cannot tokenize text|The database cannot be encrypted|Version-linked decision evidence is needed to establish what was actually assessed and released.
A team audits only successful AI actions. What is missing?|Denied, failed, and exceptional action paths|Longer success descriptions|More screenshots of normal traffic|Averaged response time|Failures and denials can reveal misuse or ineffective controls that successful paths do not expose.
An AI exception was approved by the engineer who requested it despite separation-of-duties requirements. What should happen?|Route approval to the authorized independent decision-maker|Treat technical skill as automatic authority|Delete the exception record|Backdate a generic approval|Approval authority and independence are governance controls; requester expertise does not replace them.
An assistant silently sends user requests to a new external tool. What should the owner review?|Disclosure, authorization, data flow, and supplier approval|Whether answers are shorter|The tool icon|Whether the model likes the tool|A new recipient or action path changes processing and accountability even if the interface appears unchanged.
Which audit evidence best demonstrates a kill switch works?|A controlled test showing actions stop within defined criteria|A policy saying it exists|A screenshot of its label|A vendor promise without testing|Operational controls need observed effectiveness; documented intent alone does not demonstrate performance.
An AI inventory includes only models, excluding agents and retrieval stores. What should improve?|Inventory coverage of the full deployed workflow|Model nicknames|Logo consistency|Font accessibility|Governance must account for components and capabilities that affect data access and actions beyond the model.
A risk owner leaves the company while AI exceptions remain active. What should happen?|Reassign accountability and review outstanding decisions|Leave approvals ownerless indefinitely|Delete the model inventory|Assume the vendor inherits ownership|Ongoing risk needs an accountable owner; personnel changes should trigger continuity of governance responsibilities.
A team claims an external audit eliminates the need for internal monitoring. What is the correction?|Point-in-time assurance complements ongoing oversight|An audit predicts all future changes|Monitoring is unnecessary after certification|Supplier audits cover every local use case|Audits have defined scope and timing; changing systems and local operations still require monitoring.
@2.7|Education and adoption
Developers pass an AI terminology quiz but still paste secrets into public tools. Which training change is strongest?|Role-specific exercises on real workflow decisions and safe alternatives|More acronym memorization alone|A longer attendance sheet|A generic annual slide deck only|Training should change relevant behavior; terminology recall alone does not establish safe operational judgment.
What is a stronger measure of AI awareness effectiveness than attendance?|Reduced unsafe handling in realistic follow-up exercises|Number of invitation emails|Length of the training video|Number of slides|Behavioral evidence tests whether training improves the decisions it was intended to change.
New analysts assume every AI recommendation is reliable. What should training emphasize?|Verification, uncertainty, and escalation for consequential decisions|Faster acceptance of fluent answers|Avoiding all documentation|Replacing every analyst with automation|Training should counter automation bias and teach when evidence or human expertise is needed.
Staff hide AI use because the approved alternative is unusable. What should leadership consider?|Usable sanctioned workflows plus clear risk-based guidance|Stronger punishment without alternatives|Ignoring unofficial use|Removing all inventories|Sustainable adoption combines workable safe options with understandable rules and visibility into actual use.
An executive wants the same AI training as an ML engineer. What should guide the curriculum?|Role-specific decisions and responsibilities|Identical technical depth for every role|Employee seniority|The newest model brand|Decision-makers need appropriate risk judgment while implementers need relevant technical controls; needs differ by role.
A team changes its AI tool's data sharing behavior. What should awareness owners do?|Update guidance and train affected users on the changed workflow|Reuse outdated screenshots indefinitely|Wait for the annual course|Assume settings changes are self-explanatory|Training must track material changes affecting how people handle data or authorize actions.
An AI exercise uses real customer secrets to make it realistic. What should the trainer do?|Replace them with synthetic examples preserving the learning objective|Share them only in the answer key|Publish them after class|Trust trainees to forget them|Realistic practice does not require exposing confidential data; synthetic examples can test the same judgment.
A user reports unsafe assistant advice that caused no harm. How should governance respond?|Capture the near miss and assess systemic improvements|Ignore it because no incident occurred|Punish reporting automatically|Remove the feedback channel|Near misses provide evidence about failure paths before serious consequences and support preventive improvement.
Which awareness activity best addresses deepfake payment requests?|Practice verifying requests through an independent trusted channel|Teach that familiar voices prove identity|Allow exceptions for urgent callers|Rely only on video resolution|Training should teach independent verification rather than confidence in increasingly convincing synthetic media.
An awareness program succeeds initially but unsafe use rises months later. What should be done?|Investigate causes and reinforce training with workflow controls|Declare the original training permanently sufficient|Stop measuring behavior|Increase slide count|Sustained behavior depends on current tools, incentives, and usable controls, not one-time instruction alone.
